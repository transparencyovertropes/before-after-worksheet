# Before-and-After Transparency Worksheet

A free, plain-language worksheet for showing how decision-making authority and funding change — side by side, before and after.

**[Open the worksheet →](https://transparencyovertropes.github.io/before-after-worksheet/)**

---

## What it is for

When people say authority or money is "moving," two questions usually go unanswered: **authority over what, exactly?** and **what happens to the dollars?**

This worksheet is a structure for answering both in public. You fill it in for one change you are trying to understand, and it lays out what moved, in which direction, and what is still unknown.

It does not say whether a change is good or bad. It makes the change visible so people can see it and talk about it. It is meant to be usable by anyone, regardless of what they think about the change they are describing.

Written for a general audience — the body text sits at roughly a 7th-grade reading level.

## What is in it

- **Part 1 — Who makes the decisions?** Authority broken into four jobs, tracked before and after, with a source required for every claim.
- **Part 2 — What happens to the money?** Dollar amounts, automatically calculated changes, and the strings attached to the money, which can change even when the amount does not.
- **Part 3 — What it adds up to.** A tally of what moved and how far, worked out from your entries.
- **Part 4 — What people should know.** Plain-language answers, including an explicit space for what nobody knows yet.

Plus a glossary, a worked example, and a checklist for staying fair when you publish.

There is deliberately **no overall score**. Collapsing a change into a single figure hides the decisions behind it, which is the problem this worksheet exists to counter.

## The framework behind Part 1

The four decision areas — setting the overall direction, building the organization and its supports, running the work and checking it, and doing the work — are adapted from research on how authority is divided inside organizations. In the original work the unit was a team inside a company; here it is whoever runs a public service.

These four jobs stack. Whoever sets the direction limits whoever builds the organization, and so on down. That is why the order matters: gaining authority over day-to-day work is real, and it is not the same as gaining authority over the goals. Both often get described with the same words.

Full citations are at the end of the worksheet.

## Privacy

Nothing you type leaves your device. There is no account, no tracking, no analytics, and no server. The Save button stores your work in your own browser and nowhere else. Clearing your browser data erases it, so print or download anything you want to keep.

## Accessibility

Built to WCAG 2.2 Level AA: keyboard operable, screen-reader labelled, calculated results announced, visible focus, no colour-only meaning, and a print path that will not clip long answers.
